ARRL The National Association for Amateur Radio® is following two
new federal proceedings concerning radio frequency (RF) exposure. On
September 17, 2026, the FCC and the U.S. Department of Health and Human
Services (HHS) initiated parallel comment periods on RF safety issues.
***Neither inquiry changes the current FCC RF exposure rules that apply to
all radio amateurs but could lead to changes in later proceedings if the
current rules are found to be deficient.***
<!--more-->

The FCC’s [Public Notice] is narrowly focused on specific RF exposure
issues remanded to the Commission in 2021 by the U.S. Court of Appeals.
The Commission emphasizes that it will give particular weight to
submissions reflecting specialized expertise in health and safety
matters. The HHS [Request for Information][rfi] (see also HHS [press release])
broadly seeks evidence and information concerning the potential health
effects of electromagnetic fields (EMFs), RF radiation, and wireless
technologies.

[Comments on the HHS inquiry][rfi] are due October 21, with [comments on the FCC
issues][ecfs] due by October 22.

The current rules governing RF exposure took effect on May 3, 2021, and
are discussed in the [September 2021] and [May 2023] issues of QST. The
ARRL website features additional information, including an RF exposure
calculator, the RF Safety section from the 100th Edition of *The ARRL
Handbook for Radio Communications*, an explanatory video, FAQs, and more.
ARRL has made these resources available to everyone in the amateur radio
community without regard to membership. ARRL urges all licensees to stay
compliant and safe.
